# Active Recall

Active recall means testing yourself instead of re-reading. You close the book and try to remember the key ideas, write them down, or answer questions. The effort of retrieving information is what makes it stick.

Good active recall techniques include flashcards, explaining a concept out loud as if teaching a friend (the Feynman technique), and doing past exam papers without looking at the memo first.

Note: If you cannot explain an idea in simple words, you have found a gap in your understanding. That gap is exactly what to study next.
