# Spaced Repetition

Spaced repetition means reviewing material at increasing intervals: after one day, then three days, then a week, then a month. Each review happens just before you are likely to forget, which strengthens long-term memory far more than re-reading the same notes many times in one night.

Example: After a Mathematics lesson on quadratic equations, a learner answers five practice questions the next day, five more three days later, and a short mixed quiz a week later.

Note: Cramming the night before a test can help for that test, but most of the material is forgotten within days. Spacing is what moves knowledge into long-term memory.
