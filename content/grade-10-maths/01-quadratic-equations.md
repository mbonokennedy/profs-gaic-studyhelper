# Quadratic Equations

A quadratic equation has the form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a ≠ 0. The highest power of x is 2, so a quadratic equation can have up to two real solutions (roots).

Method 1, factorising: write the equation as a product of two brackets equal to zero. If (x − p)(x − q) = 0 then x = p or x = q, because if a product is zero, one of the factors must be zero.

Example: x² − 5x + 6 = 0. Find two numbers that multiply to +6 and add to −5: they are −2 and −3. So (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0, giving x = 2 or x = 3.

Method 2, the quadratic formula: x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a. Use it when the expression does not factorise easily.

Note: Always make one side equal to zero before factorising. A common mistake is to factorise x² − 5x = −6 directly.
